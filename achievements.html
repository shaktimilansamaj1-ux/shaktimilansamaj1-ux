WORLD DAY AGAINST TRAFFICKING IN PERSONS 2026
“Trapped behind the scam”
Human trafficking is taking on new and increasingly complex forms.
Across the world, people are being deceived by fake job advertisements, transported to scam compounds, and forced into online fraud operations. Behind romance scams, cryptocurrency fraud and other cyber-enabled schemes are people who may be experiencing coercion, violence, surveillance and debt bondage. This year, the United Nations Office on Drugs and Crime (UNODC) is highlighting 'trafficking for forced criminality'; a growing form of exploitation closely connected to cybercrime, financial fraud, money laundering and corruption. Victims are not criminals. They are people being exploited.
On this World Day Against Trafficking in Persons, let us recognize the evolving forms of trafficking and strengthen our collective commitment to protecting survivors and preventing exploitation.
📢 Learn. Speak up. Share
Take a moment to learn more about trafficking for forced criminality and how people are being exploited through scam operations. Share this information with your networks to help raise awareness and ensure that more people can recognize the warning signs.
Together, we can help ensure that those trapped behind the scam are seen, protected and supported.

Shakti Milan Samaj, on 18th May 2026 conducted a Consultation and Discussion Meeting with ART counsellors,

Shakti Milan Samaj, on 18th May 2026 conducted a Consultation and Discussion Meeting with ART counsellors, health service providers and relevant CSOs to ensure smooth implementation of health-related services for people living with HIV/AIDS.
The consultation meeting concluded with a collective recognition that despite the continued commitment of health institutions and service providers, significant challenges remain in ensuring consistent viral load testing, follow-up, and comprehensive care for people living with HIV, particularly among key populations and clients outside Kathmandu Valley, highlighting the urgent need for strengthened coordination, sustained funding, decentralized services, and collaborative interventions among all stakeholders.
We are beyond grateful to have implemented this activity in collaboration with Cruiseaids Nepal and are thankful to Samata Bam for meaningful facilitation of the session.
